Important Boundaries

What Sema Nasi IS NOT

  • ＋
    Not a diagnostic clinic

    We never label, test, or diagnose you with clinical disorders. We are your peers, not clinical doctors.

  • ◌
    Not formal clinical therapy

    Our tools help you reflect and start conversations, but do not replace licensed healthcare providers.

  • ▣
    Privacy, clearly explained

    Your check-in is temporary. AI chat messages are sent to Vercel AI Gateway to generate replies; don’t include your name, school, phone number, or other identifying details.

    In a critical emergency?

    Dial Childline Kenya 116 or Befrienders Kenya 0722 178 177 for free support.

Behind the Classroom Door

Why do we keep it all bottled up?

In Kenyan schools, silence isn’t because we don’t feel pain. It is because the environment often makes vulnerability feel dangerous or shameful.

What students tell us prevents them from speakingSurvey of 480 students
Fear of being labeled “weak” or dramatic78%
Fear parents will dismiss it as “lack of prayer / discipline”69%
No private, safe guidance & counselling space in boarding61%
Guilt over school fees sacrifices made by family54%
◔

The “Vumilia tu” Culture

From dawn preps to midnight revision, we are told enduring suffering quietly is the only mark of strength. But holding boiling water doesn’t prove endurance—it just leaves burns.

▯

Boarding School Distance

Months away from home with limited 3-minute coin-box or prefect-supervised phone calls makes it hard to say anything deeper than “Natuma salamu tu.”

The Counter-Action

Breaking the silence starts with one honest sentence.

Saying “Niko overwhelmed leo” to a desk-mate is not giving up—it is the first brave step toward staying whole.
